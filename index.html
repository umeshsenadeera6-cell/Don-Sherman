The premise

More than a chef.

For more than three decades Don Sherman has moved between the pass and the stage — plating with the precision of a fine-dining chef and performing with the presence of a live entertainer.

Two disciplines that most careers keep apart, practised at the same time, in the same room, for the same audience. The result is not a chef who also sings. It is a format of its own.

The craft

A world of flavour

Six culinary traditions, each learned properly rather than borrowed — and each capable of carrying an entire evening on its own.

Culinary Heritage 01

Sri Lankan

Heat, coconut, and the smell of tempered curry leaves.

Roasted curry powder built from scratch, coconut milk added in stages, and the tempering of mustard seed and curry leaf that tells you dinner has started. Bold, layered, unapologetically spiced — and balanced by sambols that cut straight through the richness.

Signature Dishes
  • Black pork curry
  • Jaffna crab curry
  • Hoppers & lunu miris
  • Kottu roti
TRADITION 02 HINDUSTANI

Indian

Tandoor & Dum Architectural Layers
ROYAL PASS RECIPES WHOLE SPICE BLOOM
Culinary Heritage 02

Indian

Spice as architecture, not decoration.

Built in layers: whole spice bloomed in fat, ground spice added off the heat, finishing spice at the very end. From the tandoor's dry ferocity to the slow patience of a dum biryani, this is a cuisine of sequence — where the order of the spices matters more than the list.

Signature Dishes
  • Dum biryani
  • Butter chicken
  • Tandoori grill
  • Dal makhani
TRADITION 03 CANTONESE & PAN-ASIAN

Asian

Wok Hei & Umami Precision
INTENSE WOK PASS SPLIT-SECOND TIMING
Culinary Heritage 03

Asian

Wok heat, umami depth, and absolute precision of timing.

A discipline of seconds. Wok hei — the breath of the wok — exists only at the intersection of fierce heat, dry ingredients and a cook who does not hesitate. Balance is achieved with soy, vinegar, sugar and stock rather than cream.

Signature Dishes
  • Wok-fired seafood
  • Hand-pulled noodles
  • Steamed whole fish
  • Char siu
TRADITION 04 SIAM

Thai

Four Flavours in Dynamic Tension
STONE-POUNDED PASTES SWEET • SOUR • SALTY • SPICY
Culinary Heritage 04

Thai

Four flavours held in perfect tension.

Sweet, sour, salty, spicy — adjusted by taste until nothing dominates. Pastes pounded rather than blitzed, herbs added raw and late, fish sauce used with the confidence of salt. The most immediately vivid cuisine on the menu, and the least forgiving of imprecision.

Signature Dishes
  • Green curry
  • Tom yum goong
  • Som tam
  • Massaman
TRADITION 05 MALAY ARCHIPELAGO

Malaysian

Three Traditions on One Plate
SLOW-COOKED REMPAH MALAY • CHINESE • INDIAN
Culinary Heritage 05

Malaysian

Three culinary traditions on one plate.

Malay, Chinese and Indian technique layered over centuries into something singular. Rempah fried until the oil splits, coconut and tamarind pulling sweet against sour, and the slow-built complexity of a rendang cooked far past the point most cooks would stop.

Signature Dishes
  • Beef rendang
  • Nasi lemak
  • Laksa
  • Satay & peanut sauce
TRADITION 06 HAUTE CONTEMPORARY

Asian Fusion

Classical Discipline, Contemporary Plate
FRENCH SAUCE TECHNIQUE SOUTH ASIAN SPICE PROFILE
Culinary Heritage 06

Asian Fusion

Classical discipline, contemporary plate.

Fusion earns its name only when both traditions are understood first. French sauce technique carrying a Sri Lankan spice profile. Japanese restraint applied to South Asian ingredients. Fine-dining plating in service of flavours that came from a home kitchen — modern, but never rootless.

Signature Dishes
  • Curry-leaf butter sauce
  • Miso-glazed pork belly
  • Spiced dashi broths
  • Coconut & yuzu
